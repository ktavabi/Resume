\begin{cvletter}
I am writing to express my strong interest in the AI Research Scientist III position in the Office of the CTO at the Allen Institute. With a Ph.D. in Neuroscience and 15+ years of research experience, including 5+ years of experience developing and applying AI/ML models to large-scale biological and behavioral data, I am excited to contribute to the Allen Institute's mission of unlocking the complexities of bioscience and advancing knowledge to improve human health. My extensive background in electrophysiological data analysis, multimodal biological datasets, and open science contributions aligns exceptionally well with this role's focus on building large AI/ML models for biology.

\textbf{Electrophysiological and Multimodal Biological Data Expertise}

My research career has focused on developing and applying ML models to electrophysiological data—a core requirement for this position. At the University of Washington's Institute for Learning \& Brain Sciences, I developed end-to-end analytical pipelines processing high-dimensional neurophysiological datasets from 500+ participants, working extensively with MEG (Magnetoencephalography) and EEG data. I designed, trained, and evaluated ML models for speech and language processing research, analyzing neural responses using advanced digital signal processing techniques and systematically optimizing model performance through feature engineering and hyperparameter tuning. This work required developing sophisticated signal processing algorithms for noise reduction, feature extraction, and data harmonization—skills directly transferable to the multimodal biological data integration required in this role. I have extensive experience integrating neurophysiological data with behavioral assessments and clinical measurements, creating multimodal datasets that combine time-series biological data with behavioral and clinical outcomes to understand biological systems in health and disease.

\textbf{ML Model Development for Biological Systems in Health and Disease}

Throughout my career, I have developed ML models specifically for understanding biological systems, with a strong focus on health and disease applications. At The Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, I developed statistical classification systems using advanced modeling techniques to identify neural biomarkers for language impairment in autism spectrum disorders, achieving diagnostic sensitivity and specificity suitable for clinical translation. This work, which earned me an NIH Loan Repayment Award, demonstrates my ability to apply sophisticated ML and statistical methods to extract meaningful insights from complex biological data and translate research findings into clinically-relevant applications. I have applied ML techniques including PCA, time-frequency analysis, and pattern recognition to extract meaningful insights from biological signals, and developed models to predict language development outcomes from early neurophysiological markers—demonstrating the ability to use ML embeddings and representations for biological tasks.

\textbf{Open Science Leadership and Community Standards}

I am deeply committed to open science and have made significant contributions to establishing community standards for biological data processing and analysis. As a contributor to the MNE-Python ecosystem—an internationally-adopted open-source platform for electrophysiological data analysis—I helped develop standardized ML workflows and data processing pipelines that are now used by research teams globally. I co-developed MNE-BIDS, a Python-based system for organizing electrophysiological data into standardized formats, implementing automated data preprocessing and analysis pipelines that enhance code reliability and reproducibility. This experience in building community standards for scalability in developing, disseminating, and evaluating AI/ML methods for scientific problems directly aligns with the role's requirement to establish community standards across the model lifecycle. My commitment to open science extends to my publication record—10+ peer-reviewed journal articles in high-impact venues—and my practice of sharing code and methodologies to advance the field.

\textbf{Large-Scale Biological Data Pipelines and Infrastructure}

I have extensive experience building data pipelines to make biological data ML-ready, including preprocessing, normalization, and integration techniques specific to biological datasets. At the University of Washington, I developed scalable workflows handling large volumes of time-series biological data, creating end-to-end analytical pipelines for digital signal processing and data harmonization that process high-dimensional neurophysiological datasets efficiently. In my current role at Washington State Department of Corrections, I have architected scalable data science infrastructure using Python, SQL Server, and Azure cloud computing, designing robust ML pipelines that ensure efficient data processing and system reliability. While I have experience with cloud computing and scalable infrastructure, I am enthusiastic about learning and contributing to the development of petabyte-scale distributed ML infrastructure across multiple networked GPU nodes—an area where I can leverage my experience working collaboratively with software engineers to build state-of-the-art engineering infrastructure.

\textbf{Advanced ML Methods and Research Innovation}

I am excited about the opportunity to work with cutting-edge ML methods including multimodal foundation models, diffusion models, and causal representation learning. I am currently completing a DeepLearning.AI course on Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), demonstrating my commitment to staying current with advanced ML techniques. My strong foundation in traditional ML, statistical methods positions me well to learn and apply these advanced methods quickly. My track record of developing innovative computational approaches—from neural biomarker identification to diagnostic classification systems—demonstrates my ability to develop novel solutions to complex biological questions. I am particularly interested in applying these advanced methods to understand how diverse biological data types can be tokenized, embedded, and integrated to deepen our understanding of complex biological systems.

\textbf{Cross-Disciplinary Collaboration and Scientific Impact}

My experience spans academic research, clinical translation, and applied data science, demonstrating strong cross-disciplinary collaboration skills essential for working across Allen Institute teams including Brain Science, Cell Science, Immunology, Neural Dynamics, and SeaHub. I have managed over \$2 million in research grants and led interdisciplinary teams of biostatisticians, clinicians, and engineers to translate research into practical applications. I have collaborated with software engineers on open-source projects, worked with external partners on large-scale initiatives, and consistently translated complex ML models and statistical analyses into actionable insights. My strong publication record and NIH recognition demonstrate my commitment to scientific excellence and impact, while my open science contributions show alignment with the Allen Institute's values of making science accessible and advancing knowledge through collaboration.

I am enthusiastic about the opportunity to bring my electrophysiological data expertise, multimodal biological data experience, and open science leadership to the Allen Institute's Office of the CTO. My combination of deep biological data analysis experience, strong ML and statistical methods, and proven ability to translate research into impactful applications positions me to make immediate contributions to building large AI/ML models for biology and advancing the Allen Institute's mission of improving human health through open science.

Thank you for your consideration. I look forward to discussing how my experience can support the Allen Institute's innovative AI/ML initiatives in biological systems research.
